1. Lecture Scope & Applicability

Learning objective: apply the method of separation of variables to solve two-dimensional momentum-transport problems. Here, “two-dimensional” means the dependent variable depends on one spatial coordinate and on time.

Important — when separation of variables is applicable The lecture states that the method is used for linear boundary-value problems with at least one finite (bounded) spatial dimension, and with boundaries located at constant coordinate values.

The in-class recap repeats the same three ideas and adds the practical solution pattern: assume a product form such as V(x,y) = X(x)Y(y), then use an inner product and an orthonormal basis to determine expansion coefficients.

توضیح فارسی این روش زمانی مناسب است که معادله خطی باشد، دست‌کم یک راستای مکانی بازه‌ی محدود داشته باشد و مرزها در مختصات ثابت قرار گرفته باشند. ایده‌ی اصلی تبدیل یک معادله با مشتقات جزئی به چند معادله‌ی معمولی است.

2. Startup Flow Between Parallel Plates

The main lecture example is the time-dependent version of simple Couette flow. Two parallel plates are separated by a distance b. Initially the fluid and both plates are at rest. At t = 0, the bottom plate starts moving in the +x direction at constant speed v0; the upper plate remains fixed.

2.1 Assumptions and velocity field

Velocity form

vx = vx(y,t),   vy = 0,   vz = 0

The only nonzero velocity component is in the x direction, but it changes across the gap and during startup.

Forcing terms

The lecture explicitly notes that there is no x-direction pressure gradient and gravity does not act in the x direction. Therefore the viscous diffusion term is the only surviving term on the right side of the x-momentum equation.

2.2 Continuity and x-momentum equation

Continuity:   ∂vx/∂x = 0
∂vx/∂t = ν ∂²vx/∂y²,    ν = μ/ρ

This is a diffusion equation for momentum: viscous effects spread the influence of the moving wall through the finite gap.

2.3 Initial and boundary conditions

ConditionMathematical statementPhysical meaning
Initial conditionvx(y,0) = 0,   0 ≤ y ≤ bFluid initially at rest.
Boundary condition 1vx(0,t) = v0,   t > 0No slip at the moving lower wall.
Boundary condition 2vx(b,t) = 0,   t ≥ 0No slip at the fixed upper wall.
Exam Before doing any separation, write the PDE, the domain, the initial condition, and both boundary conditions. The in-class work repeatedly treats boundary conditions as the key step that determines the correct basis functions.
معنی فیزیکی در لحظه‌ی شروع، فقط دیواره‌ی پایینی حرکت می‌کند. اثر این حرکت به‌کمک لزجت به داخل سیال نفوذ می‌کند و پس از گذشت زمان کافی، پروفیل سرعت به حالت خطی پایا می‌رسد.

3. Non-dimensionalization

The handout introduces dimensionless variables to remove constants from the PDE and reduce bookkeeping errors:

ϕ = vx/v0,    η = y/b,    τ = νt/b²

Why these scales?

  • v0 is the imposed characteristic velocity.
  • b is the natural geometric length scale.
  • b²/ν is the associated viscous diffusion timescale.

Derivative transformations shown in lecture

∂vx/∂t = (v0ν/b²) ∂ϕ/∂τ
∂²vx/∂y² = (v0/b²) ∂²ϕ/∂η²

Substituting and canceling the common factor gives the parameter-free problem

∂ϕ/∂τ = ∂²ϕ/∂η²
IC: ϕ(η,0) = 0    |    BCs: ϕ(0,τ) = 1,   ϕ(1,τ) = 0
Remember The lecture says the problem can be solved dimensionally, but non-dimensionalization makes the algebra easier and reduces the chance of losing constants.
نکته‌ی مهم بی‌بُعدسازی فقط ظاهر مسئله را ساده نمی‌کند؛ مقیاس زمانی طبیعی مسئله را هم نشان می‌دهد. این مقیاس تعیین می‌کند چه زمانی اثر لزجت در کل فاصله‌ی بین دو صفحه پخش می‌شود.
Source trail: Handout 08, p. 3; lecture transcript.

4. Steady + Transient Decomposition

Because the upper wall is a finite distance away, the lecture states that the flow must approach a time-independent state at long time.

4.1 Long-time steady solution

0 = d²ϕ∞/dη²,    ϕ∞(0)=1,   ϕ∞(1)=0
ϕ∞(η) = 1 − η

The full solution is then written as a steady part minus a transient part:

ϕ(η,τ) = ϕ∞(η) − ϕt(η,τ) = (1−η) − ϕt(η,τ)

4.2 PDE, IC, and BCs for the transient term

∂ϕt/∂τ = ∂²ϕt/∂η²
IC: ϕt(η,0) = 1−η    |    BCs: ϕt(0,τ)=0,   ϕt(1,τ)=0
Important — why this decomposition is useful The original dimensionless boundary conditions are nonhomogeneous. Subtracting the steady solution creates a transient problem with homogeneous boundary conditions, which is the form used for the sine eigenfunctions.
Break 1 / Exam-style check The handout explicitly asks you to verify the transient PDE, IC, and BCs by substituting ϕ = ϕ∞ − ϕt into the original dimensionless problem.
توضیح فارسی بخش پایا شرایط مرزی غیرصفر را در خود جذب می‌کند. پس از جدا کردن آن، بخش گذرا در هر دو مرز صفر می‌شود و برای روش جداسازی متغیرها مناسب‌تر است.
Source trail: Handout 08, pp. 4–5; lecture transcript.

5. Separation of Variables

5.1 Product assumption

Assume the transient field is separable:

ϕt(η,τ) = f(η)g(τ)

Substitution into the transient PDE gives

f(η) dg/dτ = g(τ) d²f/dη²

Divide by fg:

1g dg/dτ = 1f d²f/dη² = −c²

The left side is a function only of time and the right side only of space; therefore both must be equal to the same constant. The lecture chooses −c².

Remember — sign of the separation constant For this problem, the handout/lecture uses a negative separation constant to obtain nontrivial sine modes that satisfy zero values at both ends. The “Break 2” slide asks what happens for zero or positive separation constants.

5.2 Two ODEs

dg/dτ = −c²g    ⟹    g(τ)=A exp(−c²τ)
d²f/dη² + c²f = 0    ⟹    f(η)=B sin(cη)+E cos(cη)

5.3 Boundary conditions select discrete eigenvalues

Apply f(0)=0:

E = 0

Apply f(1)=0:

sin(c) = 0    ⟹    cn = nπ

Thus the nonzero spatial modes are

fn(η) = Bn sin(nπη),    n = 1,2,3,…

The handout first notes integer values of both signs; because sine is odd, positive-index modes are sufficient for the series. The n=0 sine mode vanishes.

5.4 General transient series

ϕt(η,τ) = Σn=1∞ Dn exp(−n²π²τ) sin(nπη)

Applying the transient initial condition at τ=0 requires

1−η = Σn=1∞ Dn sin(nπη)

The remaining task is to determine the coefficients Dn; this motivates the inner-product and orthogonality material on the next slides.

توضیح فارسی شرایط مرزی فقط بعضی از مقادیر ثابت جداسازی را اجازه می‌دهند. به همین دلیل پاسخ به جای یک تابع منفرد، به صورت جمع بی‌نهایت از مُدهای مجاز نوشته می‌شود و هر مُد با زمان به صورت نمایی کاهش می‌یابد.
Source trail: Handout 08, pp. 6–7; lecture transcript.

6. Inner Product & Orthonormal Basis

6.1 Vector analogy used in the handout

The lecture connects function expansions to the familiar Cartesian vector basis. Orthogonal vectors have zero dot product, and a vector component is found by projection along a basis vector. The same idea is used to “project out” one coefficient from an infinite function expansion.

êi · êj = δij

6.2 Inner product for functions

For functions on [a,b], the handout defines

⟨f,g⟩ = ∫ab f(x)g(x)w(x) dx,    w(x) > 0

The slide lists the dot-product properties:

  1. ⟨f,f⟩ = ∫ f²w dx > 0 for nonzero f.
  2. ⟨f,g⟩ = ⟨g,f⟩.
  3. ⟨f,g+h⟩ = ⟨f,g⟩ + ⟨f,h⟩.
  4. ⟨cf,g⟩ = c⟨f,g⟩ for a constant c.

6.3 Orthogonality used for the sine basis

∫01 sin(nπη) sin(mπη) dη = { 0, m≠n ; 1/2, m=n }
Terminology note from the lecture materials The handout calls the sine family an “orthonormal basis,” but with the unscaled functions sin(nπη) the self-inner-product shown on the slide is 1/2, not 1. Operationally, the lecture uses this orthogonality relation exactly as written to extract coefficients.

The in-class lecture repeats the same projection idea for a cosine basis and emphasizes that the inner product isolates a single mode because cross terms vanish.

معنی فیزیکی و ریاضی ضرب داخلی ابزار انتخاب یک مُد مشخص از میان جمع بی‌نهایت مُدهاست. وقتی دو تابع پایه متعامد باشند، ضرب و انتگرال‌گیری باعث حذف همه‌ی مُدهای دیگر می‌شود.
Source trail: Handout 08, pp. 8–11; in-class transcript recap.

7. Coefficients & Final Startup-Flow Solution

Start from the initial-condition expansion

1−η = Σn=1∞ Dn sin(nπη)

Multiply by sin(mπη) and integrate from 0 to 1:

∫01(1−η)sin(mπη)dη = Σ Dn∫01sin(nπη)sin(mπη)dη

The handout evaluates the left integral as 1/(mπ). Orthogonality makes the right side Dm/2, so

Dm = 2/(mπ)

7.1 Final dimensionless velocity

ϕ(η,τ) = (1−η) − Σn=1∞ [2/(nπ)] exp(−n²π²τ) sin(nπη)

7.2 Interpretation

  • The first term 1−η is the steady Couette profile.
  • Every transient mode is multiplied by exp(−n²π²τ), so the transient vanishes as τ→∞.
  • Higher-n modes decay faster because the exponent contains n².
Exam Be able to move from the initial-condition series to the coefficient formula by multiplying by the matching basis function and integrating over the bounded domain.
Common Mistake Do not forget the minus sign in the decomposition ϕ = ϕ∞ − ϕt. That sign determines the initial condition for the transient term and the sign in the final solution.
جمع‌بندی فارسی پاسخ نهایی از یک بخش پایا و مجموعه‌ای از جمله‌های گذرا ساخته می‌شود. جمله‌های گذرا به مرور زمان محو می‌شوند و فقط پروفیل خطی باقی می‌ماند.
Source trail: Handout 08, p. 10; lecture transcript.

8. In-Class Problem 1: Derivative Boundary Conditions

The in-class session changes the boundary conditions while keeping the same diffusion-type PDE. This is deliberate practice in selecting the correct eigenfunction family from the boundary conditions.

k ∂²v/∂x² = ∂v/∂t,    0<x<L,   t>0
IC: v(x,0)=f(x)    |    BCs: (∂v/∂x)x=0=0,   (∂v/∂x)x=L=0
Physical boundary condition discussed in class If v is a velocity profile for a Newtonian fluid, zero velocity gradient corresponds to zero shear stress — the free-surface (liquid–gas) type boundary condition introduced earlier in the course.

8.1 Separate the variables

v(x,t)=F(x)G(t)
F″/F = (1/k)(G′/G) = −c²
F″ + c²F = 0    |    G′ + kc²G = 0

8.2 Apply derivative boundary conditions to the spatial ODE

The correct general spatial solution is

F(x)=A cos(cx)+B sin(cx)

From F′(0)=0, B=0. From F′(L)=0:

sin(cL)=0    ⟹    cn=nπ/L
Correction Note — handwritten annotation On the in-class annotated page, one line appears to write F=A cos(cx)+B cos(cx). The derivative written immediately below it and the subsequent boundary-condition step require the standard form F=A cos(cx)+B sin(cx). The corrected form is used here.

8.3 Special n = 0 mode

This is the major difference from the sine-series example in the main handout. Here n=0 gives a nonzero constant spatial mode.

Fn(x)=An cos(nπx/L),   n=0,1,2,…
n=0: G′0=0 ⟹ G0=constant
n>0: Gn(t)=exp[−k n²π²t/L²]

8.4 General series solution

v(x,t)=A0 + Σn=1∞ An exp(−k n²π²t/L²) cos(nπx/L)

8.5 Apply the initial condition and project onto the cosine basis

f(x)=A0 + Σn=1∞ An cos(nπx/L)

The in-class derivation multiplies by cos(mπx/L), integrates from 0 to L, and treats the zero mode separately:

A0 = (1/L) ∫0L f(x)dx
An = (2/L) ∫0L f(x)cos(nπx/L)dx,   n≥1

8.6 Final in-class expression

v(x,t) = (1/L)∫0Lf(x)dx + (2/L)Σn=1∞[∫0Lf(x)cos(nπx/L)dx] exp(−k n²π²t/L²) cos(nπx/L)
Exam — basis selection from BCs Dirichlet-type zero-value boundaries in the main lecture led to sine functions. Zero-derivative boundaries in class led to cosine functions and required special handling of the n=0 constant mode.
نکته‌ی فارسی تغییر نوع شرط مرزی، خانواده‌ی توابع پایه را عوض می‌کند. در شرط مشتق صفر، تابع کسینوسی طبیعی است و مُد ثابت نیز از بین نمی‌رود.
Source trail: Handout 08 In Class, pp. 3–6; in-class transcript.

The second in-class problem asks for appropriate basis functions when one boundary is a value condition and the other is a derivative condition.

k ∂²v/∂x² = ∂v/∂t,   0<x<L,   t>0,   v(x,0)=f(x)
CaseBoundary conditions on the slideRecorded in-class result
(a)(∂v/∂x)x=0=0;   v(L,t)=0cos[(n+1/2)πx/L],   n=0,1,2,…

Dimensionless variables:   ϕ=vx/v0,   η=y/b,   τ=νt/b²
Dimensionless PDE:   ∂ϕ/∂τ = ∂²ϕ/∂η²

12. Key Assumptions & Boundary Conditions

Main startup-flow assumptions

  • Velocity only in the x direction.
  • Velocity varies with y and t.
  • No x-direction pressure gradient.
  • Gravity has no x component in the equation used.
  • Newtonian constant-property form is used through constant ν.

Main startup-flow conditions

  • Initially at rest.
  • No slip at moving wall.
  • No slip at fixed wall.
  • After steady/transient split, transient BCs are homogeneous.

In-class derivative boundary condition

For the velocity interpretation discussed in class, a zero derivative at the boundary corresponds to zero shear stress at a free surface.

مرور فارسی فرض‌ها باید قبل از شروع حل نوشته شوند. در مسئله‌ی اصلی، فشار و گرانش در راستای حرکت نقشی ندارند و تغییرات سرعت فقط در فاصله‌ی بین صفحات و در زمان رخ می‌دهد.

13. Common Problem-Solving Pattern

  1. State the domain, PDE, IC, and BCs. Identify the bounded coordinate and what each boundary condition means.
  2. Non-dimensionalize when helpful. In the main example this removes all parameters from the PDE.
  3. Make boundary conditions homogeneous. If needed, split the solution into steady + transient parts.
  4. Assume a product solution. Write the transient as a spatial function times a time function.
  5. Separate. Divide by the product so a function of space equals a function of time; set both equal to a separation constant.
  6. Solve the spatial eigenvalue problem first. Apply boundary conditions to determine allowable eigenvalues and basis functions.
  7. Solve the time ODE for each mode. Keep special modes such as n = 0 when they do not vanish.
  8. Superpose all admissible modes. Write the infinite series.
  9. Use the initial condition to determine coefficients. Multiply by the matching basis function and integrate over the bounded domain.
  10. Check limits and BCs. Confirm the initial profile and long-time behavior.
الگوی حل ابتدا شرایط مرزی نوع توابع پایه را تعیین می‌کنند، سپس شرط اولیه ضرایب هر مُد را مشخص می‌کند. این ترتیب در مثال اصلی و تمرین کلاس مشترک است.

14. What I Need to Know for the Exam

Core skill 1 — know when the method applies Linear boundary-value problem; at least one bounded spatial direction; fixed coordinate boundaries.
Core skill 2 — set up the startup-flow problem Be able to write the simplified momentum PDE and the correct initial/no-slip boundary conditions, then non-dimensionalize it.
Core skill 3 — create homogeneous BCs Recognize when a steady + transient decomposition is needed and derive the transient IC/BCs.
Core skill 4 — execute separation cleanly Product form → separation constant → spatial and time ODEs → boundary-condition eigenvalues.
Core skill 5 — choose the basis from the boundary conditions Zero values at both ends lead to the sine family in the main example; zero derivatives at both ends lead to the cosine family in class; mixed conditions require shifted/half-integer modes.
Core skill 6 — use orthogonality to find coefficients Know why multiplying by one basis function and integrating isolates its coefficient. Be able to treat a nonvanishing n = 0 mode separately.
Core skill 7 — interpret the answer Identify steady and transient parts and explain why transient modes decay in time.
High-risk mistakes to avoid Wrong BC type; forgetting the steady/transient split; choosing sine/cosine before applying BCs; dropping the n=0 cosine mode; forgetting the integration interval; losing the sign in the transient decomposition.
